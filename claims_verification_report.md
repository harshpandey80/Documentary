## 2. Technical Variance & Conflict Analysis

1. **Depth Variance Resolution**:
   - Sounding echosounders from ship hulls (e.g. 1951 *Challenger II* at 5,960 fathoms / ~10,900 m, and 2010 NOAA/UNH at 10,994 ± 40 m) differ slightly from modern multibeam bathymetry (Gardner et al. 2014 at 10,984 ± 25 m) and direct in-situ submersible CTD pressure loggers (Vescovo 2019 at 10,928 m; Bongiovanni et al. 2021 at 10,935 ± 6 m).
   - *Production Guideline*: In the script, cite **10,984 meters** (or rounded 36,000 feet) as the maximum multibeam surveyed basin depth, and **10,928 meters** when referring to direct manned submersible bottom contact. Both are established in the ledger.

2. **Acoustic Anomaly Resolution**:
   - The "Western Pacific Biotwang" was documented by NOAA/OSU (Nieukirk et al. 2016) as an unidentified metallic acoustic burst (38 Hz to 8,000 Hz) recorded in the Mariana Trench Marine National Monument. In 2024, AI bioacoustic tracking conclusively identified it as Bryde's whale calls.
   - *Production Guideline*: Treat the 8-year acoustic mystery honestly—exploring how the metallic timbre deceived early listeners until machine learning resolved the biology in 2024.

3. **Meteorological Correlation**:
   - The Category 4 storm captured by Dziak et al.'s hydrophone during its July–August 2015 seafloor recording was Super Typhoon Soudelor (August 2015), not Typhoon Higos (which occurred earlier in February 2015).
