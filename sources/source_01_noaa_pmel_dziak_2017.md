# Source 01: Ambient Sound at Challenger Deep, Mariana Trench

- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.5670/oceanog.2017.240
- **Authors**: Robert P. Dziak, Joseph H. Haxel, Haruyoshi Matsumoto, Tai-Kwan Lau, Sara Heimlich, Sharon Nieukirk, David K. Mellinger, James Osse, Christian Meinig, Nicholas Delich, Scott Stalin
- **Journal**: Oceanography, Vol. 30, No. 2, pp. 186–197
- **Date**: June 2017
- **Institution**: NOAA Pacific Marine Environmental Laboratory (PMEL) / Oregon State University

### Saved Excerpt
> In July 2015, a ceramic hydrophone enclosed in a titanium pressure vessel was deployed to the seafloor of the Challenger Deep in the Mariana Trench (11°22.4′ N, 142°35.5′ E) at a depth of ~10,850 m. The instrument recorded ambient sound continuously for 23 days at a sampling rate of 32 kHz (16-bit resolution). Contrary to expectations of a silent abyss, the ambient sound field was dominated by natural geophysical noise (earthquakes with distinct T-phases), meteorological acoustics (ambient signature of a Category 4 typhoon), biological vocalizations (baleen whales and odontocetes), and significant anthropogenic contributions including commercial vessel traffic from Guam and seismic airgun exploration.
