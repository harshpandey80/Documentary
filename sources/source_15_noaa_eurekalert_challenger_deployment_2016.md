# Source 15: Mariana Trench: 7 Miles Deep, the Ocean Is Still a Noisy Place

- **URL**: https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/838186
- **Authors**: Mark Floyd / Oregon State University / NOAA
- **Date**: March 2, 2016
- **Institution**: NOAA Pacific Marine Environmental Laboratory & Oregon State University

### Saved Excerpt
> In July 2015, a team of researchers from NOAA, Oregon State University, and the U.S. Coast Guard deployed a titanium-encased hydrophone into the Challenger Deep, the deepest part of the Earth's oceans. Sinking at a controlled rate of five meters per second through the water column, the instrument settled at the bottom under 16,000 pounds per square inch of pressure. It operated continuously for 23 days until memory buffers filled. The recordings captured the rumbling of distant earthquakes, baleen whale vocalizations, ship engine thrums, and the distinct acoustic signature of a passing Category 4 typhoon. Recovery was delayed until November 2015 by persistent autumn typhoons across the western Pacific.

### Key Verified Metrics
- **Descent Speed**: 5 meters per second (controlled deployment to prevent shock damage)
- **Deployment Vessel**: USCGC *Sequoia* (WLB-215, Guam)
- **Continuous Operation**: 23 days (July – August 2015)
- **Recovery Date**: November 2015
- **Seafloor Pressure**: ~16,000 pounds per square inch (psi)
