# Source 02: A Complex Baleen Whale Call Recorded in the Mariana Trench Marine National Monument

- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1121/1.4962377
- **Authors**: Sharon L. Nieukirk, Sara Fesing Heimlich, David K. Mellinger, Robert P. Dziak
- **Journal**: The Journal of the Acoustical Society of America (JASA), Vol. 140, Issue 4, pp. EL274–EL279
- **Date**: October 2016
- **Institution**: Hatfield Marine Science Center, Oregon State University / NOAA PMEL

### Saved Excerpt
> Autonomous acoustic gliders operating in the Mariana Trench Marine National Monument recorded a distinct, complex 5-part acoustic signal dubbed the 'Western Pacific Biotwang'. The call spans frequencies from 38 Hz to nearly 8,000 Hz, with a duration between 2.5 and 3.5 seconds. It begins with a low-frequency moan (38 Hz) and culminates in a sharp, metallic high-frequency sweep reaching 8,000 Hz. The signal exhibited seasonal recurrence between autumn and spring across multiple recording stations, demonstrating acoustic characteristics previously unheard in hadal oceanic regions.

### Key Verified Metrics
- **Acoustic Call Duration**: 2.5 to 3.5 seconds
- **Minimum Frequency**: 38 Hz (low-frequency moan)
- **Maximum Frequency**: 8,000 Hz (8.0 kHz metallic sweep)
- **Signal Structure**: 5 distinct acoustic segments
- **Recording Years**: 2014–2015
