# Source 16: Ocean Ambient Noise and Deep Acoustic Propagation

- **URL**: https://scripps.ucsd.edu/research/ocean-acoustics
- **Authors**: Marine Physical Laboratory (MPL), Scripps Institution of Oceanography, UC San Diego
- **Date**: 2021
- **Institution**: Scripps Institution of Oceanography

### Saved Excerpt
> Ocean ambient sound is defined as the background acoustic field produced by an assemblage of all acoustic sources, natural and manmade. In hadal trenches below 6,000 meters, sound waves from surface shipping and meteorological phenomena penetrate downward through refraction, while high hydrostatic pressure causes sound velocity to increase steeply toward the seafloor (reaching ~1,530 m/s). This acoustic upward refraction traps sound waves within the trench floor, turning deep oceanic trenches into acoustic natural concentrators.

### Key Verified Metrics
- **Sound Velocity at Seafloor**: ~1,530 m/s (vs ~1,480 m/s at SOFAR axis)
- **Acoustic Concentrator Effect**: Upward refraction traps descending wavefronts
- **Hadal Sound Propagation Mechanism**: Refractive focusing in high-pressure trough
