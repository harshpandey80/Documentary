# Source 12: CTBTO Hydroacoustic Monitoring Network & Deep Ocean Sound Channel (SOFAR)

- **URL**: https://www.ctbto.org/our-work/monitoring-technologies/hydroacoustics
- **Authors**: Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty Organization (CTBTO) International Data Centre
- **Date**: 2022
- **Institution**: CTBTO Preparatory Commission, Vienna, Austria

### Saved Excerpt
> The CTBTO operates 11 hydroacoustic monitoring stations worldwide (including H03 Juan Fernandez, H08 Diego Garcia, and H11 Wake Island in the Pacific). Low-frequency acoustic signals (1 to 100 Hz) generated by subsea volcanic eruptions, underwater explosions, or deep earthquakes propagate through the SOFAR (Sound Fixing and Ranging) channel at approximately 1,480 to 1,500 m/s with minimal attenuation over thousands of kilometers. At hadal depths below 6,000 meters, high hydrostatic pressure increases sound speed, creating an acoustic waveguide that focuses sound energy along trench floors.

### Key Verified Metrics
- **Acoustic Frequency Band**: 1 Hz to 100 Hz (primary monitoring)
- **Deep Sound Speed**: ~1,480 to 1,530 m/s depending on temperature, salinity, and pressure
- **SOFAR Channel Axis Depth**: Typically 800 to 1,200 meters in low/mid-latitudes
- **Waveguide Focusing Effect**: High pressure gradient below 6,000 m traps acoustic energy
