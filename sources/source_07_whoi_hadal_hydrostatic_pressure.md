- **Date**: 2020
- **Institution**: Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution

### Saved Excerpt
> The hadal zone, extending from 6,000 meters to nearly 11,000 meters depth, encompasses the deepest ocean trenches formed by tectonic subduction. Hydrostatic pressure increases linearly at approximately 1 atmosphere (1.013 bar / 14.7 psi) for every 10 meters of descent. At the floor of the Challenger Deep (~10,994 m), ambient pressure reaches approximately 1,086 to 1,100 bar (over 15,750 to 16,000 psi)—the equivalent of an adult elephant balanced on a human postage stamp, or 8 tons of force per square inch. Seawater is compressed by approximately 5% at these depths. Water temperature remains near-freezing, between 1.0°C and 4.0°C.

### Key Verified Metrics
- **Hydrostatic Pressure Rate**: ~1 atm per 10 meters depth
- **Seafloor Pressure at ~10,994m**: 1,086 bar (108.6 MPa; ~15,750 psi)
- **Seawater Density Compression**: ~4.96% to 5.0% volume reduction
- **Bottom Temperature**: 1.0°C to 4.0°C (34°F to 39°F)
- **Hadal Depth Boundary**: Depths exceeding 6,000 meters
