# Source 09: Seismotectonics of the Mariana Subduction Zone

- **URL**: https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/search/
- **Authors**: United States Geological Survey (USGS) Earthquake Hazards Program
- **Date**: 2023 (Catalog Record 1900–2023)
- **Institution**: USGS National Earthquake Information Center (NEIC)

### Saved Excerpt
> The Mariana Trench marks the convergent boundary where the ancient, dense Pacific Plate subducts beneath the smaller Mariana Microplate at approximately 35 to 45 mm per year. This tectonic interface generates frequent deep and intermediate-focus earthquakes along the Wadati-Benioff zone down to 650 km depth. Ocean-bottom acoustic sensors regularly record acoustic 'T-phase' waves (tertiary waves) generated when seismic energy couples from the oceanic crust directly into the water column, propagating for thousands of kilometers through the deep ocean sound channel.

### Key Verified Metrics
- **Plate Convergence Rate**: 35 to 45 mm per year
- **Max Subduction Focal Depth**: ~650 kilometers
- **Primary Seismic Phase in Water**: T-phase (tertiary acoustic waves)
- **Plate Age**: ~170 million years (among the oldest oceanic crust on Earth)
